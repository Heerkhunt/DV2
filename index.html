From Melbourne's laneway espresso bars to Darwin's drive-through chains — exploring how café culture shapes Australian life.

Where Does Coffee Culture Run Deepest?

Coffee spending varies significantly across Australia's states and territories. The ACT and Tasmania lead in per-capita café spending, driven by dense urban café ecosystems and high disposable incomes.

Section 1 — Australia's Café Landscape

Which States Have the Most Cafés Per Person?

Raw café counts favour populous states — but normalising by population tells a different story. Tasmania and the ACT punch well above their weight, with significantly more cafés per 100,000 people than NSW or Queensland.

How Often Does Each Generation Reach for a Cup?

Coffee frequency increases with age — older Australians are the most habitual drinkers. But younger Australians are catching up fast, with daily consumption rising sharply among 25–34 year olds.

What Does Each Generation Order?

Coffee type preferences shift dramatically with age. Younger Australians gravitate toward lattes, while older generations favour cappuccinos and long blacks. The flat white holds steady across all groups — a true Australian constant.

Café or Kitchen — Where Do Australians Make Their Coffee?

Section 3 — Spending & Trends

How Has Coffee Spending Evolved?

Total coffee spending has grown dramatically since 2015 — driven primarily by café and restaurant spending. But the rise of takeaway coffee, pods and beans shows Australians are diversifying how they get their fix.
